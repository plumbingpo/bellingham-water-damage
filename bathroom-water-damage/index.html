Bathroom Water Damage: What Bellingham Homeowners Should Know

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Bellingham, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Warning Signs

  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

A Failed Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
